What is Juicefall?

Juicefall is a falling-jelly puzzle game for iPhone where you match colors, not rows. Jelly shapes drop into a narrow well; you slide and turn them, and three or more jellies of one color in a row or a column burst. Whatever falls after a match can match again, so one drop can set off a chain. On the App Store it is called Juicefall: Jelly Match Puzzle.

When does Juicefall come out?

Juicefall comes to the App Store for iPhone in November 2026. It will be free to download, with every mode free to play.

What makes Juicefall different?

Color is what counts: three or more jellies of one color, in a row or a column, burst, and full rows do nothing, so every drop is a small color puzzle. Every step of a cascade multiplies the points. A swap meter banks up to three swaps, and glowing jellies show every swap that makes a match. The shape of a match makes fruit with powers, and two fruit swapped together fuse into a kiwi with both powers, twice as strong. Calm mode has no gravity at all.

Is Juicefall free? What does it cost?

Juicefall is free to download, and every mode, the daily challenge and the lessons are free to play, with ads between games. One in-app purchase, the full game (US$2.99 in the United States; other countries see the App Store's price in their own currency), removes the ads and adds six fruit: the orange, strawberry, grapes, pineapple, dragon fruit and kiwi. It is bought once and kept forever, with Family Sharing. There is no subscription.

Does Juicefall have ads?

Yes, without the full game: a full-screen ad between games, at most once every few minutes, and a small banner on the card after a game. There are never ads during play or in the lessons. The full game removes every ad.

Does it work offline?

Yes. The whole game plays with no internet connection. Game Center scores and iCloud records catch up when the iPhone is back online.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no account and no sign-in. If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, your scores and achievements go there too.

What data does Juicefall collect?

Juicefall itself collects nothing: your records stay on your iPhone and in your own iCloud, and Game Center scores go to Apple's Game Center when you are signed in to it. Without the full game, ads come from Google AdMob, which collects some data to show and measure them; the game never asks to track you. The privacy policy has the details.

Which devices does it run on?

iPhone with iOS 26 or later. It runs at 120 Hz on iPhones with ProMotion.

What are the ways to play?

Classic speeds up every level. Calm has no gravity: shapes fall only when you drop them, and swaps are untimed. Hectic levels up twice as fast. The daily challenge is the same puzzle for everyone each day.

How do swaps work?

Landings and clears fill the swap meter. A full meter is one swap, and you can bank three; a cascade three or five steps long gives one too. When a shape lands with a swap banked, glowing jellies show every swap that makes a match: tap a jelly, then its neighbor. A swap is used up only when it makes a match, and in Calm swaps wait for you.

What do the fruit do?

Four in a row make a blueberry, which clears its row. A square of four yellow makes a banana, which knocks jellies off the board. Those two are free. Four in a column make an orange, which clears its column. An L or T shape makes a strawberry, which bursts a 3 by 3 area. Five in a line make grapes, which clear every jelly of one color. A pineapple turns every jelly of one color into another, a dragon fruit shuffles the jellies to find a match, and two different fruit swapped together fuse into a kiwi with both powers, twice as strong. Two of a kind fuse into a super fruit, three times as strong.

Is Juicefall suitable for children?

It is rated 4+ on the App Store, with no chat, and its ads are limited to content for general audiences. It is made for all ages rather than for children in particular.

Is it accessible?

Every color in Juicefall is a shape of its own (a heart, a square, a drop, a circle, a star, a hexagon and a flower), so colors can be told apart without seeing them, and VoiceOver can play through its actions.

Which languages is it in?

English, French, German, Japanese, Portuguese (Brazil) and Spanish.
